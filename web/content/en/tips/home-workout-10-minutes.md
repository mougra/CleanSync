Ten minutes in the morning sounds like a compromise, but in practice it is the most sustainable option: this workout is easy to repeat on weekdays and weekends alike.

## Why 10 minutes beats an hour

A big workout needs time, kit, a shower and motivation — and is easily postponed “until tomorrow”. A short one needs none of that: it can be done in pyjamas, before breakfast, and leaves no excuse to skip.

The main effect of the first weeks is not muscle but the habit of moving. Once ten minutes feel normal, you can make it longer.

## A simple routine without equipment

Warm-up: two minutes — head turns, shoulder circles, bends, marching on the spot.

Main part, 40 seconds each, two rounds:

1. Squats — knees tracking over the toes, back straight.
2. Wall or knee push-ups — a comfortable range.
3. Glute bridge — lift the hips.
4. Knee plank — 20–30 seconds.
5. Forward folds — gently, no bouncing.

Cool-down: a minute of stretching and calm breathing. If an exercise feels hard, shorten it and add a pause — doing less without pain beats doing more with it.

## How not to quit after the first week

- Put the workout at the same time: after waking up or after coffee.
- Make the step ridiculous: for the first days, do five minutes of the routine, not all of it.
- Tick it off in [habits](/en/habits) — the streak helps keep the rhythm.
- Do not sacrifice sleep: if mornings are not yours, move the workout to the evening. The time does not matter, regularity does.

## First-week checklist

1. Pick three exercises from the list and do them for 30 seconds each.
2. Set a phone reminder for a time when you are definitely at home.
3. Tick the workout in the app and look at your streak at the end of the week.
4. If you miss a day, come back the next one — no “starting on Monday”.

When the workout becomes normal, add something for the mind: [10 minutes of a foreign language](/en/tips/language-10-minutes-a-day) works well next to it.
