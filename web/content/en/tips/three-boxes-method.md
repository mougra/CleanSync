The main reason sorting things out gets stuck is that **every single item is decided on its own,
with no limits**. That is where "it might come in handy" comes from, and a box that stands there
for three years.

## How it works

Take three containers and label them:

- **Keep** — what you have used in the past year.
- **Give away** — whole and working, but not needed by you.
- **Bin** — broken, out of date, without its pair.

Then the timer takes over: **thirty minutes for one shelf**. Whatever did not fit into a decision moves
to "Keep" until next time: that is fine, you do not sort the whole home in one go.

## Rules that speed things up

- Do not read or examine: a decision is made in two seconds.
- "Give away" is not a storage room. What you have collected goes out within a week, or the box goes back
  into the cupboard.
- The same things live together: three chargers in one place, not in three drawers.
- Empty containers are clutter too. Keep as many jars and bags as you actually use.

## What to do with "give away"

- Things in good condition — to charity funds or to neighbours via the building chat.
- Books — to a book swap, gadgets — to recycling, medicines — only to pharmacy disposal.
- If there is no one and nowhere to give it to, binning it is more honest: a thing on the balcony
  helps no one.

> A useful question for every item: "if I were moving tomorrow, would I pack this?"
> The answer "no" — that is the decision.
