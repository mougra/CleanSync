Anyone can wash a floor, but not ruining the surface does not always follow: laminate swells with
water, parquet goes dull from alkali, and tiles are left with streaks from dirty water. The
difference comes down to one rule — how much water and which product the material allows.

## The general rule: less water

A microfibre cloth wrung out almost dry and a neutral product solve nine cases out of ten. Water
should not be left in puddles or run into the joints. Start at the far wall and work towards the
door, and change the cloth or the water as soon as it turns grey: dirty water does not wash
a floor, it spreads the dirt.

## Laminate and vinyl

- **A damp microfibre cloth only.** Wring it out so that nothing drips from it.
- **A neutral product** for laminate, or an ordinary solution of mild soap. Acids and alkalis eat
  into the protective layer and the surface loses its shine.
- **No steam and no bucket of water.** Hot steam and flooded joints lift the edges and the
  locking joint comes apart. That cannot be repaired.
- **No abrasives and no melamine sponges,** even for scuff marks from shoes: they leave matt
  patches.
- **Felt or felt pads under furniture legs.** Scratches on laminate come from moving a chair,
  not from washing.
- **Vinyl and rigid vinyl** cope with water more easily, but the product should still be neutral:
  aggressive chemicals strip the protective lacquer.

## Tiles and porcelain stoneware

Here you can use more water, but not just any chemical.

1. **Warm water and mild soap** for everyday washing. A mop with a spray bottle is handier than
   a bucket.
2. **Clean the grout with bicarbonate of soda.** A paste of soda and water plus a stiff brush.
   Limescale comes off with a solution of citric acid; after that, rinse the grout with clean
   water.
3. **Do not mix acid with bleach.** That is not 'double strength' but a dangerous reaction.
4. **Acid is not for every surface.** It eats into polished marble, travertine and enamel; for
   those, use a neutral product only.
5. **Wash matt tiles more often.** Water marks show on them, but at least they are not slippery.
6. **Renew the grout every couple of years.** While it is intact, water does not get under
   the tiles.

## Parquet and solid wood

- **A special product for wood only** and a slightly damp cloth. Alkali, spirit and all-purpose
  cleaners raise the grain and strip the varnish.
- **No puddles and no wet cloths.** Water gets into the seams between the boards and leaves dark
  marks.
- **Felt pads on all the furniture** and a mat at the entrance: sand works like sandpaper.
- **Once every year or two** parquet is refreshed with oil or a polish suited to the finish. That
  costs less than sanding it back.
- **Deal with stains at once,** blotting rather than rubbing: the longer the liquid sits, the
  deeper the mark.

## What is worth remembering

Before choosing a product, remember what the surface under your feet actually is. For laminate
and parquet the main enemy is water, for tiles it is acid on the wrong surface, and for all of
them it is abrasives and a dirty cloth. If in doubt, start with the mildest option and test it
on a hidden patch.
