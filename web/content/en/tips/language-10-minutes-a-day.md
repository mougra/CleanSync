A foreign language is a classic example of a habit people quit not because it is hard, but because the step is too big: “study for an hour” gets postponed, while “ten minutes” gets done.

## Why short sessions work

Memory likes spaced repetition. Ten minutes every day give a hundred repeats in two weeks, while a two-hour marathon once a month gives only two touches of the material. The habit matters more than the volume: it provides regularity.

## How to choose one language and one format

1. Pick one language and one goal for the month: “order food”, “read the news”, “hold a conversation”.
2. Take one main tool — an app, a textbook or a course — and do not switch every week.
3. Add one source of live speech: a podcast, a series with subtitles, songs. It is not a lesson but a background that helps you recognise words.

## Ritual: place, time, reminder

A habit holds on an anchor to an existing action. “After breakfast, while the coffee brews” works better than “in the evening, when there is time”.

- Set a reminder in the app and tick the session off in [habits](/en/habits).
- Keep the material in sight: the app on the first screen, the book on the desk.
- If ten minutes do not happen, do five. The main thing is not zero.

## How to measure progress

Not “learned the language” but measurable steps: twenty new words, one dialogue, five minutes without subtitles. Once a week look at the streak and note what worked. Misses do not cancel progress — [how to return after a setback](/en/tips/how-not-to-quit-a-habit) is a separate article.

A language, like order at home, is built from small repeated actions.
