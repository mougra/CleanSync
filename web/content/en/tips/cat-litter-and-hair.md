Cat hair and the litter tray are the two things that make a home with a cat 'all furry' and
'smelling of something'. They have to be dealt with differently: the tray is seen to every day,
while hair is removed as it appears, not once a month when the mood takes you.

## The litter tray: the most important spot in the home

- **Scoop out the clumps twice a day.** Morning and evening, with a scoop into a bag. The smell
  does not come from the litter but from what has been lying in it for a day.
- **Top up the litter rather than waiting for a full change.** A layer of 4–6 cm: in a thin layer
  the clumps break up and dirty the bottom, and washing the bottom is harder than throwing the
  litter away.
- **Do a full change once every one to two weeks.** Clumping litter lasts longer than wood-based
  litter, but it absorbs smells too.
- **Wash the tray with warm water and household soap.** Bleach and air fresheners put the cat
  off, and it will start going outside the tray.
- **Once a month, wash the floor and the mat under the tray.** Urine gets under the sides and
  stays there for a long time — that is the smell you usually notice in the hallway.

If there are several cats, there should be more trays than cats: one for each plus one spare.
Otherwise you get conflict and puddles in unexpected places.

## Hair on furniture and clothes

A dry cloth does not remove hair, it just chases it round in circles. What works is anything that
catches it:

1. **A rubber glove or mitt.** Go over the sofa with slightly damp rubber — the hair gathers into
   clumps that are easy to lift off by hand.
2. **A lint roller** — for clothes, cushions and the throw. A reusable one is rinsed under water
   and lasts for years.
3. **A vacuum cleaner with a turbo brush** — for the rug and the carpet. Without the brush the
   hair stays in the pile.
4. **A damp microfibre cloth** — windowsills, shelves, skirting boards and the spot by the cat's
   favourite place.

## Fabrics, the cat bed and the scratching post

- Throws and sofa covers go in the wash at 60 °C once a fortnight, not 'when the hair shows'.
- Shake the cat bed out on the balcony and wash it according to the label; choose models with
  a removable cover.
- Vacuum a sisal scratching post rather than washing it: water makes it soggy and it starts
  to smell.
- Fabric mice and balls go in the wash in a laundry bag; wipe plastic toys with a damp cloth.

## The floor, rugs and cloths

- **Wash the floor around the tray every time** you clean the tray: there is always a little
  scattered litter there.
- **A mat in front of the tray** catches the granules from the paws — wash it together with the
  other fabrics.
- **Vacuum with a turbo brush** twice a week: you cannot see the hair in the carpet, but it is
  there.
- **A separate cloth for the cat's places,** washed at 60 °C after every clean. Otherwise the
  smell travels round the flat with the cloth.

## What is worth remembering

Hair and smell are not about how often you do a deep clean, but about where things live. A closed
bin for the litter, a washable cover on the sofa, a scoop next to the tray and a place for the
cat's toys do more than one heroic weekend a month.

> A quick test: run your hand over the sofa in a dark room with a torch. If the pile
> 'goes silver', it is time to reach for the rubber glove.
