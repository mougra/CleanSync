A child's room fills up with toys not because the child is untidy, but because the things have no
permanent place. The request 'tidy your room' does not work here any better than it does for
adults: it is too big. What works is a short task, a timer and low shelves.

## Fifteen minutes for everyone

Announce the time: 'we tidy for fifteen minutes, then we go and have tea'. During that time the
adult tidies their own part — the windowsill, the floor, the table — rather than standing over
the child. Each person has their own task and it ends in plain sight: the basket is full, the
floor is clear, the table is empty.

## Baskets instead of a single box

- **Five baskets with pictures.** Dolls, cars, building blocks, craft things, 'to sort out'.
  Stick on a picture or write a label: a child who cannot read yet goes by the drawing.
- **Open low shelves.** Anything you have to reach for and lift a lid off does not get used.
- **A bag for small pieces.** Blocks and puzzle pieces are easier in a clear container with
  a lid: you can see what is inside and it does not spill.
- **Rotation instead of a mountain.** Put some of the toys away in the store cupboard and bring
  them out once a month. After a week they are interesting again, and the room gets a chance
  to breathe.
- **One basket for paper and bits.** Drawings, sweet wrappers and broken odds and ends go
  straight in there, without discussion.

## The play area and the floor

Agree where playing is allowed and keep that place free of furniture. A rug or a small piece of
carpet marks out the area, and the small pieces stay on it rather than all over the flat.
Wash the floor in a child's room twice a week: that is where children sit and lie down.

## Safe products

- Warm water with soap or a solution of bicarbonate of soda is enough for the table, the
  windowsill and the floor.
- Use bleach and strong products when the child is not there, then rinse the surface with clean
  water and air the room.
- Never mix products together, not even 'to get it cleaner'.
- Keep bottles of household chemicals above the child's reach and separately from food.
- Wash soft toys at 40 °C in a laundry bag, and wipe cloth books with a damp cloth.

## Five minutes every evening

- Before bed only the toys on the floor are picked up — into the baskets, without sorting or
  organising.
- On the desk only what is needed tomorrow stays: the school bag, the uniform, a book.
- Clothes for the morning are got ready the night before and hung on a chair, rather than
  hunted for in the wardrobe.
- Wrappers and bits go out straight away, not 'we will collect them later'.

## Common mistakes

- **Tidying up instead of the child.** It is quicker, but next time they will not join in:
  'they will redo it anyway'.
- **Tasks with no end.** 'Tidy everywhere' is never finished and gives no sense of a finish line.
- **Tidying as a punishment.** 'You are not going out until you tidy up' turns order into
  a punishment.
- **Too many toys within reach.** It is hard for a child to choose and put away when the baskets
  are overflowing from the start.
- **New purchases without letting go.** A new toy car arrives — one old one goes to the store
  cupboard or to a good home.
