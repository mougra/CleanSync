Laundry seems simple until you ruin a favourite jumper. Most problems are solved by
**sorting before loading** and the right temperature, not by an expensive detergent.

## Three baskets

- **Lights and bedding** — 60 °C, ordinary powder.
- **Darks and colours** — 30–40 °C, colour gel.
- **Delicates and wool** — 30 °C, the "wool" cycle, no spin at maximum.

Separately: do not wash towels with fluffy items, or jeans with T-shirts (zips
leave pulls).

## Temperature: a short cheat sheet

- **60 °C** — bedding, towels, kitchen textiles: these need hygiene and grease removal.
- **40 °C** — everyday clothes, children's things.
- **30 °C** — darks, synthetics, anything with prints.
- **Cold** — wool, silk, sportswear membranes.

## Drying and ironing

- Shake the item out before you hang it up: half the creases disappear straight away.
- Shirts and trousers go on hangers while still wet, then ironing is hardly needed.
- Do not overdry: overdried fabric creases more.
- Dry with air circulation: in a closed bathroom things start to smell.

## What makes clothes last longer

- Turn prints inside out and do up the zips.
- Use less detergent than the pack says: excess foam rinses out poorly.
- Once a month, an empty 60 °C cycle with citric acid against smells.
- Do not leave wet laundry in the drum: the smell appears within a couple of hours.

> If things smell after washing, the problem is almost always the machine, not the powder:
> check the door seal and the drain pump filter.
