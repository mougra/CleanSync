A big clean feels daunting because of its size, so it gets postponed to the weekend — and then
takes half a day. The opposite logic works better: **small daily steps** instead of rare bursts.

## The fifteen-minute ritual

Set a timer and move round the rooms without lingering:

1. **Kitchen (5 minutes).** Clear the clutter off the worktop, rinse the sink, sweep up the crumbs.
2. **Living room (5 minutes).** Cushions back in place, throw folded, things returned to their zones.
3. **Hallway (5 minutes).** Shoes in pairs, coats on the hook, bag back where it lives.

As soon as the timer goes off — stop. Even if something is left: this is not a big clean,
it is its prevention.

## Why it works

- **The threshold.** Fifteen minutes is not frightening to start, two hours is.
- **Feedback.** You see the result every evening, and that keeps the habit going.
- **Less build-up.** Clutter does not have time to turn into "sort out the whole wardrobe".

## Common mistakes

- Starting too many zones at once. Begin with one room.
- Cleaning "to perfection" instead of "good enough".
- Giving up the habit after missing a day. One missed day means nothing, regularity is what matters.

> The most useful question in the evening: "what will take five minutes and remove the main source
> of irritation?" Usually it is the sink, the table or the hallway.
