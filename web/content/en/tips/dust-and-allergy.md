Dust is a mixture of fabric fibres, skin, paper and particles from outside. Some of it settles, some of it
hangs in the air, and **a dry cloth simply moves it from place to place**. Hence the difference between
"wiped" and "there is less dust now".

## What actually works

1. **Wet cleaning instead of dry.** A slightly damp microfibre holds dust rather than chasing it about.
2. **Top to bottom.** Cupboards and shelves first, then windowsills, and only then the floor: whatever
   has settled above will fall down anyway.
3. **A vacuum cleaner with a HEPA filter.** An ordinary vacuum without a filter throws the fine fraction back out.
4. **Washing textiles.** Curtains, throws and pillowcases are the main collectors: 60 °C once a month.
5. **Window screens.** Half of the "household" dust arrives from outside.

## What does not help, even though it seems logical

- **A dry broom and feather dusters** — they lift dust into the air, where it hangs for hours.
- **Air fresheners and "anti-dust" sprays** — they mask the smell, the dust stays.
- **Silicone polishes** — the surface shines, but it attracts dust more.
- **Cleaning with an open window on a windy day** — the dust comes back before the cloth dries.

## If there is an allergy sufferer at home

- Wash the floor every other day, and either remove the rugs or clean them more often.
- Pillows and duvets go into the wash on a schedule, not "when we remember".
- Check the humidity: at 40–50% dust settles, at lower levels it hangs in the air.
- Get rid of dust collectors: collections, heavy curtains, open shelving full of small items.

> A simple test: run a white napkin along a shelf a day after cleaning. If it is grey,
> the problem is the textiles or the ventilation, not the cloth.
