The request "tidy your room" works neither for children nor for adults: it is too big
and too vague. What works is **one zone, one clear result and a timer**.

## What helps

1. **Fifteen minutes for everyone.** A timer turns cleaning into a game rather than a punishment.
2. **A task to suit the age.** For a toddler — put the toys in a box, for a schoolchild — wipe the table
   and put things away, for a teenager — a zone of their own, all of it.
3. **A visible result.** An empty box, a clean table, a neat row of shoes: the child should
   see that they have finished.
4. **Together, not "go and tidy up".** The adult cleans their own zone at the same time.
5. **No redoing it in front of the child.** Otherwise next time they will not start: "they will redo it anyway".

## How to share out the zones

- **Shared zones in turn:** today the kitchen is one person's, tomorrow another's.
- **Personal zones are always your own:** desk, bed, shelf.
- **Pets:** feeding and bowls are an excellent first responsibility.

## What ruins everything

- Cleaning as a punishment: "you are not going out until you tidy up".
- Tasks that cannot be finished: "tidy up everywhere".
- Promises not kept: once it is agreed, it is done, and that goes for adults too.

> The phrase that works best: "Your job is the table and the toys, mine is the kitchen, we meet back in
> fifteen minutes". Children join in willingly when a task has a boundary.
