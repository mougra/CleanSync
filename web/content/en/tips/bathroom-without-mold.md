Mould does not come from nowhere: it needs **moisture, warmth and still air**. A bathroom has all
three, so fighting it means fighting damp first of all.

## Three habits

1. **Squeegee the water off the walls.** After a shower, run a squeegee over the tiles and the glass —
   twenty seconds, and the surface dries without marks.
2. **Leave the door and the curtain open.** While the bathroom dries, the air has to move.
   The curtain is best spread out: water stands longest in the folds.
3. **Air the room or run the fan.** If you have an extractor, let it work for
   fifteen minutes after a shower.

## If mould has already appeared

- On grout: a paste of bicarbonate of soda and water, a brush, then dry it. Peroxide or vinegar are for
  more stubborn spots.
- On sealant: if the silicone has turned black, surface cleaning will not help — the sealant has to be replaced.
- On the curtain: wash it at 60 °C with ordinary powder once a month.

## What not to do

- Mixing cleaning products "to boost the effect": chlorine with acid gives off toxic gas.
- Sealing over old grout with new sealant — the mould will carry on underneath.
- Keeping towels in a closed bathroom: they do not dry and start to smell.

> The cheapest remedy for mould is a draught after a shower. Everything else is just the consequence
> of forgotten moisture.
