A balcony is a buffer between the street and the home: street dust, pollen, poplar fluff and
everything that is 'just put here for now' ends up on it. So cleaning starts not with a cloth
but with empty space.

## First, take everything out

Empty the balcony completely: boxes, jars, sledges, cardboard boxes, the drying rack, the plants.
Sort everything into three groups:

- **needed this season** — stays;
- **needed, but not now** — into closed containers;
- **broken and other people's things** — out, not 'just in case'.

The rule: if a thing has not been used for a year, it will not be needed next year either.
Check jars and boxes separately: the empty ones take up half the balcony.

## Dust and washing

1. **Vacuum the corners and the joints.** Dry dust once wet turns into dirt that you then have
   to scrub off.
2. **Walls and ceiling — from the top down.** Soapy water and a soft-bristled brush.
3. **Frames and the windowsill — with a toothbrush in the seams.** That is where the black film
   collects.
4. **Wash the glass on a cloudy day.** In the sun the solution dries in seconds and leaves
   a streak.
5. **The floor — last, with two cloths.** The first lifts the worst of the dirt, the second
   finishes the job. Change the water more often than seems necessary.
6. **Let everything dry** and only then bring the things back in.

## What you will need

A bucket of warm water with mild soap, a stiff-bristled brush for the floor, a toothbrush for
the seams of the frames, a squeegee for the glass, two microfibre cloths and gloves. A vacuum
cleaner with a narrow nozzle — before water, not after. As for products, soap and bicarbonate
of soda are enough: aggressive chemicals are not needed on a balcony.

## Glazing: the order that saves an hour

A clean window comes not from the product but from the order of your movements. Wet the glass
with the solution, go over it with a soft sponge or a wash mitt, pull the water off with
a squeegee from top to bottom, and overlap each following pass onto the edge of the previous
one. Wipe the squeegee dry after every pass — otherwise it smears the dirt back on. It is easiest
to wash the inside vertically and the outside horizontally: that way you can see at once where
a mark has been left.

## Storage by season

- **Winter things** — sledges, skis, ice skates, Christmas decorations in closed boxes.
- **Summer things** — the swing, the sun lounger, garden furniture, the clothes airer.
- **Containers, not boxes.** Cardboard goes soggy and attracts insects; plastic with a lid keeps
  dust and damp out.
- **Anything above chest height** goes in closed cupboards: less dust settles there.
- **Do not store anything that fears frost and sun:** paint, household chemicals, electronics,
  shoes, books, furniture varnish.
- **Fix the shelves to the wall,** rather than stacking them on top of each other: a balcony is
  damp, and a stack of boxes soon slides apart.

## What is worth remembering

It is best to wash the balcony twice a year, in spring and autumn, at the same time as washing
the windows throughout the flat. Better still, do not turn it into a storeroom at all: then
cleaning takes half an hour instead of a whole weekend.
