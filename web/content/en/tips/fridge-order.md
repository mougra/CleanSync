Food goes to waste not because we are careless, but because **we do not see what we have**.
Opaque bags and deep shelves hide food before it spoils.

## Three rules

1. **If it is visible, it gets eaten.** Move grains, leftovers and sliced food into clear containers.
   What you cannot see, you will not remember.
2. **One shelf for "eat first".** At eye level — whatever needs finishing in the next
   two days: opened jars, leftovers, anything perishable.
3. **Nothing goes in front of an older date.** New to the back, old to the front. This shop-shelf
   rule works at home too.

## The order of sorting

- Take out one shelf at a time and sort it completely: throw away, move, wash.
- Check the dates on sauces and dressings — they go off unnoticed.
- Keep only what you really need every day in the door: the rest of the space there is the warmest
  and the least suitable for storing anything.

## Little things that help

- Write the date on containers with leftovers in marker: "cooked on the 6th".
- Keep one tub for vegetables that are starting to wilt: they turn into soup or a stew.
- Once a week, before you shop, look inside the fridge and plan the menu around what is left.

> The one-minute rule: before you put the shopping away, spend a minute binning anything out of date
> and wiping the shelf. That way a stocktake never turns into a big clean.
