Most attempts at a big clean end on the second day: too much at once, no visible result,
no energy left. A different scheme works — **zones by day and short regular tasks in between**.

## Why by zones, not by floors

- A zone is finite: you can see where you started and where you finished.
- The result is visible straight away: the hallway was clean on day one already.
- Tiredness does not build up: two hours a day instead of eight on Saturday.

## The plan for the week

1. **Hallway and living room** — clear the surfaces, dust, wash the floor.
2. **Kitchen** — worktops, one cupboard, appliances.
3. **Bedroom** — bedside tables, wardrobe, bedding.
4. **Bathroom** — tiles, fittings, products.
5. **Sorting** — one cluttered zone using the three-box method.
6. **Floors and surfaces** — rugs, floors, furniture.
7. **Final touches** — the places you forgot and one cosy detail.

## What keeps you from giving up

- **No "while I am here".** You started the wardrobe — do not start the storage above it, or the day
  will end in chaos.
- **The timer matters more than perfection.** Better to finish with an imperfect skirting board
  than to give up on day three.
- **Regular minutes.** While the week is running, five minutes in the morning and fifteen in the evening
  keep the home in order and stop the clutter coming back.
- **Rest in the plan.** If you missed a day, just shift the plan rather than doubling the load.

> At the end of the week, do something nice for your home: flowers, a candle, a new throw.
> That is how cleaning gets linked to something good rather than to tiredness.
