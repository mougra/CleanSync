The yard and the garden need two different rhythms: one thing is what repeats every week, and
quite another is the work that is done twice a year. Mix them into one list and you get
a permanent race and the feeling that 'everything has been neglected'.

## Every week

- **Sweep the paths and the porch.** Ten minutes, and dirt is not carried into the house.
- **Clear the leaves off the lawn.** Not in a layer but straight away: under a thick layer the
  grass suffocates.
- **Check the drainpipe and the storm drain.** One gutter blocked with leaves — and the water
  runs down the wall.
- **Water and look over the plants.** At the same time you spot pests and broken branches.
- **Clear up after the animals** and take the rubbish out: in summer this is a matter of one day.
- **Check the lights and the path.** A cracked slab and a blown bulb are more dangerous than
  they look.

## Once a month

1. **Wash the paths and the area by the entrance.** A stiff brush and a soapy solution; green
   film comes off with a solution of bicarbonate of soda. Wash on a cloudy day so that the
   solution does not dry out.
2. **Trim the shrubs and the hedge.** Better little and often than cutting back half of it once
   a year.
3. **Check the tools.** Tighten loose handles, take the rust off with a brush and oil them.
4. **Water and loosen the flower beds,** pull the weeds before they flower, otherwise in a month
   there will be twice as many.
5. **Check the hose and the attachments** for leaks at the joints.

## An order that saves time

Start with the rubbish and the leaves, then the paths, then the plants, and the tools right at
the end. That way dirt does not get back onto the slabs you have just washed. Keep the bags,
gloves and dustpan by the way out to the yard, not in a shed at the far end. If the work takes
more than an hour, split it into two goes: the heavy work in the morning, the watering in the
evening.

## Once a season

**Spring.** Clear the winter rubbish and the remains of the leaves, check the drainage, feed the
lawn, wash the paths after the winter, sort through the seeds and seedlings. If grit or salt was
spread in winter, wash the slabs by the entrance especially thoroughly.

**Summer.** Watering in the mornings rather than in the heat of the day, mowing once every one
to two weeks, treatment against pests, checking the furniture and the swing for loose fixings.

**Autumn.** Leaves to a schedule rather than in one go; covering the heat-loving plants; shutting
down the watering and draining water from the barrels and hoses; putting the garden furniture
away under covers; the last mowing a little shorter.

**Winter.** Clearing snow from the paths, grit in moderation, a check after strong wind for
broken branches over the path. Give the time you have freed up to the tools: wash them, dry them,
oil the metal, sharpen the cutting parts, put them away handles up in a dry place.

## What is worth remembering

The same principle works in the yard as in the house: short, frequent actions cost less than one
big campaign. Ten minutes a week on the paths and the drains saves a whole day's work in spring,
and one shed sorted out in autumn saves you hunting for the secateurs in May.
