You cannot stuff a mattress, a pillow or a thick duvet into the drum, but that does not mean they
are clean. Dust, sweat, skin oil and dust mites live there rather than in the sheet. Let us look
at what you can do yourself.

## The mattress

1. **Vacuum both sides.** An upholstery attachment, medium power, slowly over the whole surface
   and especially along the seams.
2. **Bicarbonate of soda for half an hour.** Sprinkle a thin layer, leave it for 20–30 minutes,
   then vacuum it off. Bicarbonate of soda takes up smells and moisture.
3. **Blot stains rather than rubbing them.** A slightly damp cloth with mild soap, working from
   the edge towards the centre so that no ring is left. You must not soak it through: damp will
   stay inside and a smell will appear.
4. **Let it dry completely** and air the room. A damp mattress undoes all your work.
5. **Turn it every three months** — both head to foot and side to side. That way it sags evenly.
6. **A zip-up protector** solves half the problem: it can be washed while the mattress stays
   clean.

## Pillows

- **Read the label.** Synthetic — 40 °C, feather and down — 60 °C with tennis balls, which knock
  the filling back into shape.
- **Wash them in pairs,** so that the drum does not get thrown off balance.
- **Dry them right through.** A feather pillow takes a day or more to dry; damp inside it smells
  and goes mouldy.
- **The test for replacing it:** fold the pillow in half and let go. If it does not spring back
  by itself, the filling has clumped and it is time for a new one.
- **Change them every one to two years,** even an expensive one. This is a consumable, not
  something for a decade.
- **Between washes,** hang the pillows out on the balcony for a couple of hours: sun and air
  reduce the damp.

## The duvet and bed linen

- **Pillowcases every three to four days,** a full set every one to two weeks. This is the most
  frequent wash in the home, and it does most for how you feel.
- **A temperature of 60 °C** for cotton and linen: that is what is needed against mites.
- **The duvet depends on the filling.** Synthetic can go in the machine at 40 °C on a gentle
  spin, wool is hand wash or dry clean only, down needs 60 °C with tennis balls and a long
  drying time.
- **Rinse twice.** Traces of detergent in a duvet attract dust and leave a smell.
- **Store it in a case.** Dust on bedding comes out of the wardrobe just as readily as out of
  the air.

## If someone in the home has allergies

- Change the linen twice a week and wash it at 60 °C rather than at 30.
- Take the decorative cushions and the heavy bedspread off the bed: they collect dust.
- Once a month go over the mattress with a vacuum cleaner and an upholstery attachment.
- Air the bedroom for ten minutes before bed, even in winter.

## What is worth remembering

Bedding is cleaned not when it looks dirty but to a schedule: linen once a week, bicarbonate of
soda on the mattress once a month, pillows washed once a season. Half an hour a month adds years
to the life of a mattress and noticeably reduces morning sneezing.
