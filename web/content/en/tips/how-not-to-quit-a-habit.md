Willpower is a poor foundation: it runs out by the evening and disappears entirely on holiday. Habits rest on a system you can repeat without heroism.

## A habit is a system, not willpower

Any stable habit has three parts: a cue (what starts it), an action and a reward (what you get). Anchor the cue to an existing action: after coffee, before the shower, after tidying the kitchen. Then you do not need to remember — the environment reminds you.

## The tiny step and the two-minute rule

A new habit should start with an action you cannot fail to do: two squats, one page, one exercise in an app.

- Make the step ridiculous: not “a workout” but “put on trainers”.
- Allow yourself to stop after the tiny step — often you will continue on your own.
- Tick it off in [habits](/en/habits): the streak and chart show that you are not starting from zero every time.

## What to do after a setback

1. Do not move the start “to Monday”: one day decides nothing, while the pause reinforces the thought “I quit”.
2. Return with the tiny step, not the full version.
3. Look at the cause: inconvenient time, too much volume or no reminder? Fix one thing.

## How to return the next day

Make the return as cheap as possible: prepare everything for the habit the night before, set a reminder and tick off even the shortest version. If you want a system, start with one habit from the [catalogue](/en/habits) and add the second only a couple of weeks later.

Order at home is also built from small returns, not from a perfect marathon.
